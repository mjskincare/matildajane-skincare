Why pH Matters

Salicylic acid only works below pH 4.

Above pH 4, salicylic acid sits as an inactive salt. Below pH 4, it converts to its active acid form and can penetrate the lipid barrier of the follicle.

Buffered to pH 3.5 — the sweet spot for full bioavailability without unnecessary irritation.

Lipid-Soluble

BHAs go where AHAs can't.

Glycolic acid clears the surface — but congestion happens deeper, inside oil-filled follicles. Salicylic acid is lipid-soluble, so it dissolves into sebum and works on blockages from the inside out.

Made in Aotearoa

Kānuka — NZ's quiet antimicrobial.

The lesser-known cousin of mānuka, with comparable antimicrobial activity and a softer aromatic profile. Sustainably wild-harvested from the East Cape.
